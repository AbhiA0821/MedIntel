## Clinical Definition
Normal core body temperature typically ranges from 36.5°C to 37.5°C (97.7°F to 99.5°F).

- **Normothermia**: 36.5°C – 37.5°C.
- **Low-Grade Pyrexia**: 37.6°C – 38.4°C.
- **High Fever / Severe Pyrexia**: Core body temperature > 38.5°C (101.3°F).

## Clinical Risk & Pathophysiology
Body temperature above 38.5°C represents an acute febrile reaction, most commonly driven by pyrogenic cytokines during systemic infection, sepsis, or inflammatory crises.

1. **Metabolic Burden**: For every 1°C elevation in body temperature above normal, basal metabolic rate increases by approximately 10–13%, driving up cardiac output, oxygen consumption, and carbon dioxide production.
2. **Hemodynamic Effects**: Fever induces peripheral vasodilation and tachycardia. When combined with hypoxemia or pre-existing cardiac disease, high fever accelerates clinical deterioration.
3. **Sepsis Warning**: Temperature > 38.5°C combined with tachycardia (> 90 bpm) or tachypnea (> 20/min) meets Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS) criteria.

## Recommended Clinical Actions
- Obtain blood cultures prior to antimicrobial administration if systemic infection is suspected.
- Administer antipyretic medication (e.g., Acetaminophen / Paracetamol) as clinically indicated.
- Maintain adequate intravenous or oral hydration to prevent volume depletion.
- Monitor vital trends every 15–30 minutes until fever stabilizes below 38.0°C.
