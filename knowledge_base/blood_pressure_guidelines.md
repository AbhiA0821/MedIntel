## Clinical Definition
Blood pressure measures systemic arterial lateral pressure during ventricular contraction (systolic) and relaxation (diastolic).

- **Normal BP**: Systolic < 120 mmHg and Diastolic < 80 mmHg.
- **Stage 2 Hypertension**: Systolic 140–159 mmHg or Diastolic 90–99 mmHg.
- **Severe Hypertensive Urgency/Crisis**: Systolic > 160 mmHg or Diastolic > 100 mmHg in inpatient setting.

## Clinical Risk & Pathophysiology
Systolic BP exceeding 160 mmHg subjects vascular endothelium and target organs to elevated mechanical shear stress.

1. **Target Organ Risk**: Acute severe hypertension risks acute coronary syndrome, aortic dissection, hypertensive encephalopathy, acute renal injury, or cerebrovascular hemorrhage.
2. **Left Ventricular Wall Stress**: High AFTERLOAD increases left ventricular workload, increasing risk of acute pulmonary edema or myocardial ischemia in vulnerable patients.
3. **Synergistic Abnormalities**: When high BP coincides with high HR or fever, cardiac workload compounds exponentially.

## Recommended Clinical Actions
- Confirm cuff size and repeat blood pressure measurement in both arms after 5 minutes of rest.
- Assess patient for acute target-organ symptoms (headache, visual changes, chest pain, shortness of breath).
- Administer ordered oral or intravenous antihypertensive medications as clinically prescribed.
- Notify attending cardiology team if BP remains > 160/100 mmHg despite resting protocols.
