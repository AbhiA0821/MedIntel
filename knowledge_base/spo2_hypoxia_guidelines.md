## Clinical Definition
Peripheral capillary oxygen saturation (SpO2) measures the percentage of oxygen-saturated hemoglobin relative to total hemoglobin.

- **Normal SpO2**: 95% – 100% on room air.
- **Mild Hypoxemia**: 90% – 94% on room air.
- **Critical Hypoxia**: SpO2 < 90% (or < 88% in chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure like COPD).

## Clinical Risk & Pathophysiology
A drop in SpO2 below 90% indicates significant hypoxemia leading to impaired cellular respiration and tissue hypoxia.

1. **End-Organ Impact**: Brain and myocardial tissues are highly sensitive to oxygen deprivation. Prolonged hypoxemia increases risk of ischemic organ injury, confusion, arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest.
2. **Compensatory Response**: Systemic reflex response induces compensatory tachycardia (increased HR) and tachypnea (increased RR) to maintain tissue oxygen delivery.
3. **Common Triggers**: Pulmonary embolism, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), pneumonia, severe atelectasis, hypoventilation, or acute heart failure.

## Recommended Clinical Actions
- Administer supplemental oxygen immediately via nasal cannula or simple face mask to target SpO2 ≥ 92% (or 88–92% in COPD).
- Ensure patent airway, verify sensor alignment and signal quality on digit/earlobe.
- Elevate head of bed to 30–45 degrees to maximize diaphragm excursion.
- Perform arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis and chest radiography if hypoxemia persists.
