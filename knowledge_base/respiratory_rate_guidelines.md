## Clinical Definition
Respiratory rate measures the number of breaths taken per minute (breaths/min or rpm).

- **Normal Adult RR**: 12 to 20 breaths/min.
- **Moderate Tachypnea**: 21 to 24 breaths/min.
- **Severe Tachypnea**: > 24 breaths/min.

## Clinical Risk & Pathophysiology
Respiratory rate > 24 breaths/min is one of the most sensitive early clinical indicators of acute inpatient physiological deterioration.

1. **Early Alarm Indicator**: Tachypnea often precedes heart rate or blood pressure instability in pulmonary edema, sepsis, and pulmonary embolism.
2. **Work of Breathing**: Rapid shallow breathing reduces effective alveolar ventilation, increases dead space ventilation, and causes respiratory muscle fatigue.
3. **Metabolic Acidosis Compensation**: Tachypnea is a primary respiratory compensatory mechanism (blowing off CO2) for underlying metabolic acidosis (e.g., lactic acidosis in sepsis, diabetic ketoacidosis).

## Recommended Clinical Actions
- Evaluate chest wall expansion, accessory muscle use, and breath sounds bilaterally.
- Synchronize with SpO2 monitor to assess oxygenation efficiency.
- Position patient upright to reduce work of breathing.
- Notify medical team for arterial blood gas evaluation if tachypnea persists above 24/min.
