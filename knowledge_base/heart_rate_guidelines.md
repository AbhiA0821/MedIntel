## Clinical Definition
A resting heart rate in adults normally ranges from 60 to 100 beats per minute (bpm).

- **Normal Resting HR**: 60–100 bpm.
- **Tachycardia**: Resting heart rate persistently above 100 bpm.
- **Severe Tachycardia**: Resting heart rate exceeding 120 bpm, indicating acute systemic stress, compensatory mechanism for hypoxia, infection, hyperthyroidism, or primary cardiac arrhythmia.

## Clinical Risk & Assessment
When heart rate exceeds 120 bpm in a resting inpatient:
1. **Myocardial Oxygen Demand**: Sustained tachycardia increases cardiac workload and myocardial oxygen consumption while shortening diastolic filling time.
2. **Associated Causes**: Compensatory response to tissue hypoxia (low SpO2), systemic fever/infection (elevated body temperature), hypovolemia, or severe pain.
3. **Monitoring Priorities**: Continuous ECG monitoring, pulse oximetry synchronization, assessment of hemodynamic stability, and evaluation for acute coronary syndrome or systemic sepsis.

## Recommended Clinical Actions
- Confirm patient positioning (supine or semi-Fowler's position).
- Cross-reference with SpO2 and Temperature to evaluate for compensatory shock or sepsis.
- Perform 12-lead ECG if HR remains > 120 bpm without obvious extra-cardiac triggers.
- Notify attending cardiologist or medical team if accompanied by chest discomfort or hypotension.
